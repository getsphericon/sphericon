# An Identify through the collect key is a claim until the customer's server proves it

> Amends [[0002-one-contact-identity-absorb-the-tracking-profile]] (Identify moves a Visitor only
> with a proof), [[0006-custom-fields-are-declared-by-use]] (the collect key declares none),
> [[0072-identify-writes-a-contacts-core-attributes]] (a claim only fills),
> [[0075-destinations-are-contact-attributes-or-bindings]] and
> [[0076-presets-parse-inbound-and-the-core-reads-what-it-means]] (a Visitor's Binding reaches no
> Contact), [[0086-a-form-submit-binds-only-an-anonymous-visitor-and-only-fills-a-known-contact]]
> (a submit fills no alias key), [[0088-phone-consent-from-an-on-site-form-comes-from-a-message-the-person-sends]]
> and [[0160-a-tracked-links-identity-token-binds-only-an-anonymous-visitor]].

## Context

The collect key is public: it sits in the page of the customer's site, so whoever loads the page can
call Identify with any `subjectId`, email or phone. Identify was built for the customer's own code
after a sign-in (ADR 0086): it moved the Visitor to the Contact the keys resolved, filled the
Contact's empty alias keys, overwrote its core attributes (ADR 0072), declared a Custom field for
every other trait (ADR 0006), and the Telegram chats started from the Visitor reached that Contact
(ADR 0075, ADR 0076). A form submit (ADR 0086) and a Link identity token (ADR 0160) already treat
what comes through the collect key as unproven. Platforms that route messages to a device sign the
identity on the customer's server and check the signature before the device is tied to a person.

## Decisions

- **An Identify is a claim unless it carries an Identity token.** A claim finds or creates the
  Contact by its alias keys. A Contact it creates gets everything it carries. A Contact that exists
  gains no alias key, and only the core attributes and Custom fields it has no value of: a claim
  never replaces or clears. A claim binds only a Visitor that is anonymous or already that
  Contact's, and stitches the Visitor's anonymous Events onto it; a Visitor of another Contact stays
  where it is. A claim records no Duplicate candidate (ADR 0128).
- **A proven Identify carries an Identity token**: a JWT the customer's server signs with HS256
  under the Workspace's Identity secret. It names the alias keys it proves (`sub` for the
  `subjectId`, `email`, `phone`, at least one) and an `exp` at most 24 hours ahead. The keys of a
  proven Identify are the token's: a key the call also sends must be the same once normalized. A
  proven Identify does what Identify did before: it fills alias keys, writes core attributes
  (ADR 0072) and moves the Visitor. A token that does not verify (a bad signature, an `exp` past or
  too far ahead, a Workspace with no Identity secret) refuses the call; it never falls back to a
  claim, so a broken integration shows.
- **The Identity secret** is the Workspace's, generated by sphericon when an Owner or Admin asks for
  it, stored under the Instance's Encryption key as a signing secret is, and readable by them.
  Regenerating it replaces it at once: it is what a leaked secret calls for, and a token lives a day
  at most. A Workspace has none until one is generated.
- **A Visitor's Binding reaches no Contact.** Sends, Conditions, the Contact's card and inbound
  messages see only a Contact's own Bindings. A chat becomes a Contact's when a Start token issued to
  the Contact starts it: through the APIs, a merge tag, or the tracker when its request carries an
  Identity token that verifies. A proven Identify also hands the Visitor's Bindings to the Contact it
  proves and records `telegram.bound` for each. A claim, a form submit and a Link identity token hand
  over nothing. A Start token on SMS or WhatsApp (ADR 0088) follows the same rule.
- **The collect key declares no Custom field.** Identify writes a trait only to a Custom field the
  Workspace has defined (in the app, through the API or an import) and drops the rest, as it drops
  an invalid core attribute (ADR 0072), proven or not: the token proves who, not what.

## Consequences

- Identify takes the claim rules, and the Visitor's Bindings stop reaching its Contact.
- The Identity secret is a table of its own, one row per Workspace, read and regenerated on the
  tracking settings by those who hold `tokens:manage` and erased with the Workspace. Identify and
  the tracker's start link request take an `identityToken`; the tracker keeps the one of the page's
  latest Identify in memory and sends it with the start link request. A site calls the tracker's
  `reset` when the person signs out: it forgets the token and starts a Visitor of its own, so the
  next person on the browser starts a chat for no one and is not stitched onto the one before.
- A browser shared by two people keeps a residual: the chats started on it before a sign-in go to
  whoever proves a sign-in next, as a device's push subscription does on the platforms that sign.

## Rejected

- **Refusing every Identify without a token**: a site with no sign-in (a newsletter, a guest
  checkout) would lose the stitching of the browsing before an email is typed.
- **A Workspace switch that enforces the proof**: a claim is safe on its own, and a switch is a second
  behaviour for every reader of Identify.
- **A hash of one identifier**: it proves one key while the call asserts up to three, and it never
  expires.
- **Signing only the `subjectId`**: an email sent beside it would still be filled onto the proven
  Contact.
- **A key pair whose public half sphericon keeps**: the customer manages a key pair to gain only
  that a copy of the database cannot forge tokens, which a sealed secret already prevents.
- **Remembering on the Visitor that its Contact was proven**: it outlives the sign-out, and a chat the
  next person on the browser starts would go to the previous Contact.
